# Multiplayer Gamble Roguelike — Paper Playtest Kit 01

- Status: **Draft facilitator protocol; not yet executed.**
- Use only after game cards, payout tables, clue generation, item effects, and recovery rules are locked for the test.
- Target: three groups, ideally 2–4 players per group. A 6-player session should be added if available.

## 1. Test objective

Find out whether the game creates:
- meaningful decisions rather than repeated clicks;
- understandable risk/reward choices;
- discussion without one player controlling the whole group;
- different viable approaches across runs;
- a desire to replay after a win or loss.

Do not ask players whether the game is “fun” after every turn. That invites politeness and noisy opinions. Observe choices first, then ask neutral questions.

## 2. Materials required

- Five stage cards with target, strain, route options, and one shared wager rule.
- Risk Contract card with Safe / Balanced / Jackpot odds and gross returns.
- Vault card with each step's probability and cash-out multiplier.
- Signal Room card with exact clue-generation rules, clue fees, and payout table.
- Blind Draft card with Favor-token rule and item-pick order.
- Ten item cards with activation cost, use limit, drawback, and eligible event types.
- Bank/strain tracker, item inventory, route-vote slips, secret risk-vote slips, timer, and observer sheet.
- A printed rules reference with gross return clearly distinguished from net profit.

Do not improvise missing rules during a session. If a rule is unresolved, mark that test block invalid and fix the design first.

## 3. Facilitator setup (2 minutes)

1. Tell participants that chips are virtual and have no real-money value.
2. Explain the shared bank, stage target, strain rule, and simultaneous secret vote.
3. Tell players that the goal is to complete the run, not to maximize one player's personal score.
4. Give each player the same one-page rules reference.
5. Do not explain an optimal strategy; the test is measuring whether players can infer one.

## 4. Teach-back check (maximum 3 minutes)

Ask each participant to explain, in their own words:
- what happens to the bank on a win and on a loss;
- what a displayed gross multiplier includes;
- how a risk vote is resolved;
- what happens when a stage target is missed;
- how and when an item can be used.

Record misunderstandings before correcting them. If more than one participant in a group misunderstands the same rule, mark the rule/UI as unclear.

## 5. Run script (12–20 minutes target)

For each of five stages:

1. Show the bank, target, strain, two route options, and visible odds.
2. Give the group up to 30 seconds to discuss routes.
3. Each player privately submits a route vote and a risk vote where applicable.
4. Reveal votes together and apply the published median/tie/AFK rule.
5. Let the team choose any permitted item/support action.
6. Lock one shared wager; resolve the event using the printed probability table.
7. Announce the outcome and show gross payout, net bank change, target result, and strain change.
8. If the run enters recovery, offer only the written recovery choices.
9. After a stage clear, let the team draft an item from the printed choices.
10. Observer records discussion length, who spoke, whether the final choice was understood, and whether anyone was inactive.

Do not give strategy hints unless the rules are impossible to continue. If help is required, record the exact prompt and the stage.

## 6. Observer sheet (one row per stage)

Record:
- Stage number and starting bank.
- Route offered and selected.
- Votes by risk tier, selected tier, and whether players understood the result.
- Item used and why the group selected it.
- Outcome, bank change, target clear/miss, and strain.
- Discussion time in seconds.
- Number of players who contributed a reason.
- Any player who was silent, confused, or disengaged.
- Whether the decision felt consequential to the group (1–5).
- Whether the result felt explainable/fair (1–5).
- Any moment described spontaneously as tense, funny, frustrating, or satisfying.

## 7. Post-run questions

Ask each player privately before group discussion:
1. Which decision mattered most, and why?
2. Was there a point where you felt you had no meaningful choice?
3. Did the risk vote represent the group's preferences fairly?
4. Did any player dominate or block the group's decisions?
5. Which item changed your plan rather than simply making numbers bigger?
6. What rule was hardest to understand?
7. Would you play another run with the same group? Why or why not?
8. What would you remove before adding anything new?

Then ask the group:
- What strategy did you think you were pursuing?
- When did that strategy change?
- Was the loss/win mostly understandable from decisions or did it feel arbitrary?
- What made you want to continue or stop?

Do not lead with “Was that fun?” or “Did you like the items?” Ask for concrete moments first.

## 8. Initial pass/fail criteria

Treat these as prototype gates, not market success claims.

- At least 70% of participants say they would play another run.
- Median run duration is 12–20 minutes.
- No participant is inactive for more than 20% of the session.
- At least two of three groups can explain the reason for their major risk choices.
- No more than one repeated rule clarification per stage across a group.
- At least two distinct strategies appear across the three groups.
- No one item or game is selected automatically in every applicable state.
- Participants can explain why a loss occurred without believing the result was secretly changed.

A small sample cannot establish market-wide appeal. Report individual counts and quotes alongside percentages; do not hide a 2/3 result behind a polished chart.

## 9. Stop and revise conditions

Stop the test and revise if:
- the payout table is inconsistent or the clue probabilities cannot be explained;
- players cannot tell whether a payout includes the stake;
- the shared-bank vote feels arbitrary or consistently ignores a minority;
- one player can intentionally force a catastrophic bet;
- a player has nothing useful to do after a loss;
- one item/route is obviously correct every time;
- the group spends more time waiting for the next turn than making decisions.

## 10. Current status

- This kit has not been used with human participants.
- It is not evidence that the game is fun.
- Do not start implementation just because a paper test protocol exists. Complete the probability, item, recovery, and strategy gates first.
